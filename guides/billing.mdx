## Overview

Stripe Billing helps you turn one-time purchases into recurring revenue. You can create subscription plans, charge by usage, send invoices, and recover failed payments with dunning workflows. Use it when you need predictable revenue, flexible pricing, or a billing system that can scale from a small launch to a global operation.

Stripe is designed for multiple billing models, including flat-rate subscriptions, seat-based pricing, usage-based pricing, and mixed plans. You can combine Billing with Payments, Radar, and customer tools so your revenue operations stay in one place.

## Subscription management

Subscription management is the foundation of recurring billing. In Stripe, you define prices, attach them to customers, and keep a record of each subscription state change. That includes trial periods, plan changes, cancellations, pauses, and renewals.

You should model your pricing around how customers actually buy. For example, a SaaS product may offer monthly and annual plans, while a service business may need seat-based access with add-ons. Stripe lets you manage all of those patterns without rebuilding your billing logic every time your pricing changes.

### Common subscription patterns

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Flat-rate" icon="package">
    
    Use this when every customer pays the same recurring amount.

    - Good for simple SaaS tiers
    - Easy to explain on your pricing page
    - Works well with monthly or annual cadence

  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Seat-based" icon="users">
    
    Use this when the total bill depends on the number of active users.

    - Add or remove seats as customers grow
    - Recalculate the invoice automatically
    - Keep customer access aligned with payment status

  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Hybrid" icon="layers">
    
    Use this when you need a base subscription plus add-ons.

    - Combine a recurring fee with usage charges
    - Support plan upgrades without changing your billing architecture
    - Make pricing flexible for larger customers

  </Tab>
</Tabs>

### Setup example

A subscription usually follows a simple lifecycle:

1. Create a product and price.
2. Attach the price to a customer subscription.
3. Monitor status changes such as trialing, active, past due, and canceled.
4. Listen for events so your app stays synchronized with Stripe.

<CodeGroup tabs="JavaScript,cURL">
  ```javascript
const subscription = await stripe.subscriptions.create({
  customer: "cus_example123",
  items: [
    {
      price: "price_example_monthly",
    },
  ],
  trial_period_days: 14,
  expand: ["latest_invoice.payment_intent"],
});
  ```

  ```bash
curl https://api.example.com/v1/subscriptions \
  -u sk_test_YOUR_API_KEY: \
  -d customer=cus_example123 \
  -d "items[0][price]"=price_example_monthly \
  -d trial_period_days=14
  ```
</CodeGroup>

## Metered billing

Metered billing lets you charge customers based on what they actually use. This is useful for APIs, infrastructure products, analytics tools, and any service where consumption varies from month to month.

Stripe Billing supports usage tracking so you can record events throughout the billing period and invoice customers at the end of the cycle. You can also design pricing with volume-based or tiered usage models when your business needs more than a single unit rate.

### When to use metered billing

- You bill by request, minute, gigabyte, or transaction.
- Customer usage changes often.
- You want invoices to match real consumption.
- You need to combine fixed subscription fees with variable charges.

<Expandable title="How to think about usage records" default-open="false">
  
  Record usage consistently and as close to the source as possible. If your service measures API calls, send counts from the system that knows the true number of calls. If you measure storage or compute, choose a source of truth that you can audit later.

  Keep the units simple. Customers understand requests, users, seats, and hours more easily than internal technical metrics.
  
</Expandable>

### Usage example

```javascript
await stripe.subscriptionItems.createUsageRecord(

  {
    quantity: 2500,
    timestamp: Math.floor(Date.now() / 1000),
    action: "set",
  }
);
```

## Invoices

Invoices are the record of what a customer owes and why. Stripe can generate invoices automatically for subscriptions, one-time adjustments, and usage charges. You can also send manual invoices when you need custom payment terms, net due dates, or approval workflows.

Use invoices to improve clarity for your customers. Include line items that explain the charge, payment terms, tax details, and the next billing date. Good invoices reduce support tickets and make revenue operations easier to reconcile.

### Invoice workflow

<Steps>
  <Step title="Create invoice items" icon="list">
    Add subscription charges, usage charges, or one-time adjustments.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Finalize the invoice" icon="check-circle">
    Lock the invoice so totals and line items are ready for payment.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Collect payment" icon="credit-card">
    Charge the customer automatically or send the invoice for manual payment.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Track payment status" icon="bar-chart">
    Monitor paid, open, voided, and uncollectible states.
  </Step>
</Steps>

### Invoice design tips

- Keep line item names readable.
- Separate recurring and one-time charges.
- Show taxes and discounts clearly.
- Use invoice email messages to explain changes in pricing or usage.

<Callout kind="tip">
If customers often question bills, add clearer product naming and a short usage summary to each invoice.
</Callout>

## Dunning

Dunning is the process of recovering failed payments. When a card expires, a bank declines a charge, or a payment method requires customer action, Stripe can retry payments and notify customers based on your settings.

A good dunning strategy reduces involuntary churn without creating a poor customer experience. Start with a clear retry schedule, then adjust based on how your customers respond. For example, you may want immediate retries for temporary network failures and slower follow-up for soft declines.

### Recommended dunning practices

| Practice | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Retry with a schedule | Gives failed payments another chance to succeed |
| Notify customers early | Helps customers fix expired cards or missing funds |
| Track past due subscriptions | Lets you intervene before churn becomes permanent |
| Use clear email copy | Reduces confusion during payment recovery |

<Expandable title="What to monitor" default-open="false">
  
  Watch the number of failed payment attempts, the time between failure and recovery, and the share of subscriptions that move from past due to canceled. Those signals tell you whether your retry strategy is working.

  If you see many recoveries after the first retry, your payment failures may be temporary. If recoveries are rare, you may need better customer reminders or a different retry schedule.
  
</Expandable>

## Best practices

- Choose a pricing model before you launch, then keep it stable enough for customers to understand.
- Test subscription upgrades, cancellations, and renewals before you go live.
- Keep usage measurement accurate and auditable.
- Send invoices with enough detail to explain what changed.
- Treat dunning as a recovery flow, not just a retry setting.

If you want a billing system that can support subscriptions, metered plans, invoices, and payment recovery in one platform, Stripe gives you a consistent way to do it.
