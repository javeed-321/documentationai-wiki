## Getting started

Zendesk helps you set up support quickly, connect the right channels, and use AI to speed up resolutions. If you are just getting started, focus on account setup first, then add channels and integrations, and finally train AI on the kinds of issues you handle most often.

<Callout kind="info">
  If you want a guided overview first, use the demo and onboarding resources from Zendesk before you configure production workflows.
</Callout>

<ExpandableGroup>
  <Expandable title="How do I start a new Zendesk account?" default-open="true">
    Create your account, choose the service workspace that fits your team, and complete the basic profile details for your organization. Then confirm your support email, set your time zone, and invite the first agents or administrators.

    Start with the essentials only. You can add workflows, channels, and automation after the team can log in and see tickets.
  </Expandable>

  <Expandable title="What should I configure first after signup?" default-open="false">
    Set up your support channel, define your business hours, and review notification settings. After that, add the main forms, ticket fields, and routing rules you need for day one.

    If you support multiple teams, keep the first setup simple. You can refine roles, triggers, and views once the team is comfortable with the workspace.
  </Expandable>

  <Expandable title="How do I know the account is ready for agents?" default-open="false">
    You are ready when agents can sign in, receive tickets, reply from the right channel, and see the fields they need to resolve issues. A quick test ticket should move through the full workflow without manual workarounds.

    If anything feels incomplete, check permissions, channel verification, and ticket routing before you launch.
  </Expandable>
</ExpandableGroup>

## AI configuration

Zendesk AI is designed to help you automate common questions, assist agents, and improve over time. The safest way to begin is to train it on a narrow set of requests, review the results, and expand only after the answers look accurate.

<Callout kind="tip">
  Start with a small set of high-volume questions. Clear examples help AI learn faster and reduce noisy results.
</Callout>

<ExpandableGroup>
  <Expandable title="How do I train an AI agent in Zendesk?" default-open="true">
    Use your existing help content, solved tickets, and common question patterns as training material. Group similar requests together so the AI can recognize intent and suggest the right response.

    Review early drafts carefully. Tight examples and clean content usually give better results than a large but messy knowledge base.
  </Expandable>

  <Expandable title="What kind of content should I use for training?" default-open="false">
    Use short, accurate articles, clear question and answer pairs, and real examples of what customers ask. Focus on issues that happen often and have a stable, repeatable answer.

    Avoid outdated instructions or conflicting policies. AI performs best when the source material is simple and current.
  </Expandable>

  <Expandable title="How do I improve AI quality over time?" default-open="false">
    Review unresolved conversations, low-confidence suggestions, and edited replies. Update the underlying content when you see repeated confusion.

    Treat AI improvement as an ongoing loop: measure, fix, retest, and expand. That keeps the system useful as your support volume changes.
  </Expandable>
</ExpandableGroup>

## Channels and integrations

Zendesk lets you bring conversations from multiple channels into one place and connect the tools your team already uses. Good channel setup reduces missed messages, and good integrations reduce copy-paste work.

<ExpandableGroup>
  <Expandable title="Which channels can I connect first?" default-open="true">
    Start with the channels your customers use most, such as email, chat, voice, or messaging. Connect one or two channels first so you can verify routing, agent availability, and response behavior before you add more.

    Once the basics work, expand to the rest of your customer touchpoints.
  </Expandable>

  <Expandable title="How do integrations help my team?" default-open="false">
    Integrations bring external data into the workflow so agents can resolve issues without switching tools. That can include account details, order status, IT systems, or internal service data.

    The goal is simple: fewer tabs, less manual lookup, and faster resolutions.
  </Expandable>

  <Expandable title="What should I check when a channel is not working?" default-open="false">
    Confirm the channel is authenticated, the routing rule is active, and the correct group or team can receive messages. Then test with a real inbound message and watch where it lands.

    If the problem continues, review permissions and any recent configuration changes before you make larger updates.
  </Expandable>
</ExpandableGroup>

## Resolution workflow

Zendesk is built to move issues from first contact to resolution with less manual effort. If your workflow feels slow, check the basics first: assignment, visibility, and the steps a ticket must pass through before it closes.

<ExpandableGroup>
  <Expandable title="How do I speed up ticket handling?" default-open="true">
    Use clear routing, the right ticket fields, and automation for repetitive steps. Keep your views focused so agents can find the work that needs attention now.

    Small workflow improvements usually have the biggest impact. Remove extra steps before you add new ones.
  </Expandable>

  <Expandable title="How do analytics help with onboarding?" default-open="false">
    Analytics show whether tickets are coming in cleanly, where delays happen, and which channels create the most work. That helps you spot setup issues early.

    Use the first reports to confirm that routing, staffing, and response times match your expectations.
  </Expandable>

  <Expandable title="How do I know the workflow is set up correctly?" default-open="false">
    Run a test case from start to finish. Submit a request, confirm assignment, reply from the right channel, and close the ticket using your expected process.

    If the path breaks anywhere, fix that step before you go live.
  </Expandable>
</ExpandableGroup>

## Security and governance

Zendesk includes enterprise-grade security and AI governance so you can support customers and employees with more confidence. During setup, make sure access, approvals, and data handling match your internal policy.

<ExpandableGroup>
  <Expandable title="What should I review before going live?" default-open="true">
    Check user roles, permission boundaries, and who can change workflows or AI settings. Then confirm that your team knows which data should and should not be shared in support conversations.

    A quick review now prevents avoidable mistakes later.
  </Expandable>

  <Expandable title="How do I control who can change AI settings?" default-open="false">
    Limit configuration access to the people who own support operations or platform administration. Use role-based access so day-to-day agents can work tickets without changing the AI setup.

    Keep change ownership clear. That makes audits and troubleshooting much easier.
  </Expandable>

  <Expandable title="What is the best way to handle sensitive customer data?" default-open="false">
    Store only the data you need, restrict access to sensitive fields, and review your sharing rules regularly. If your workflow uses integrations, confirm that each connection follows your security requirements.

    When in doubt, keep the workflow simple and expose less data to more people.
  </Expandable>
</ExpandableGroup>
