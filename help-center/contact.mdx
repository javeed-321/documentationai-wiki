## Contact and support

If you are stuck, start with the fastest path to resolution: search the help center, ask the community, or submit a support ticket if you need direct help from Zendesk.

Use this page as a quick guide to the three main support paths:

  <Card title="Submit a ticket" icon="help-circle" href="#">
    
    Contact Zendesk support when you need account-specific help, product troubleshooting, or a case you want tracked.
    
  </Card>
  <Card title="Community forums" icon="users" href="#">
    
    Search for answers from other Zendesk users, admins, and experts who have solved similar issues.
    
  </Card>
  <Card title="Enterprise account management" icon="shield" href="#">
    
    Reach your account team for rollout planning, billing questions, and guidance for larger deployments.

<Callout kind="info">
  Zendesk support works best when you share a clear summary, the affected account, the steps you already tried, and screenshots or error text.
</Callout>

### Before you reach out

Try to collect the details that speed up a fix:

- Your account name and subdomain
- The feature or workflow that is blocked
- The exact error message, if you see one
- The time the issue started
- The browser or device you used

## Submit a support ticket

Use a support ticket when you need help with a specific Zendesk account or need Zendesk to investigate behavior you cannot resolve on your own. This is the best option for urgent issues, product defects, and configuration questions tied to your setup.

The ticket flow is simple:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Describe the issue" icon="message-circle">
    
    Write one short summary that explains what is broken and who is affected.
    
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add the key details" icon="file-text">
    
    Include account information, timestamps, and any steps that reproduce the problem.
    
  </Step>
  <Step title="Submit and track" icon="check-circle">
    
    Send the request and monitor the case for updates from Zendesk support.
    
  </Step>
</Steps>

### What to include in the ticket

A strong ticket helps support respond faster and with fewer follow-ups.

| Include | Why it helps |
| --- | --- |
| Clear summary | Makes the issue easy to route |
| Account name | Identifies the affected environment |
| Reproduction steps | Helps support verify the behavior |
| Screenshots or screen recordings | Shows exactly what you see |
| Error text | Removes guesswork |
| Impact level | Helps prioritize urgent cases |

<ExpandableGroup>
  <Expandable title="How do I write a good ticket?" default-open="true">
    
    Keep it short and specific. Start with the problem, then add the steps to reproduce it and the result you expected.
    
  </Expandable>
  <Expandable title="When should I use a ticket instead of the community?" default-open="false">
    
    Use a ticket when the issue is account-specific, time-sensitive, or needs Zendesk to review data that is not public.
    
  </Expandable>
  <Expandable title="Can I add attachments?" default-open="false">
    
    Yes. Attach screenshots, logs, or recordings that show the issue clearly. Remove sensitive data first.
    
  </Expandable>
</ExpandableGroup>

## Community forums

Use the community when you want practical advice, examples, or confirmation from other Zendesk users. It is a good place to compare approaches, learn best practices, and see how other teams solved similar problems.

Community answers are especially useful for:

- Setup questions
- Workflow ideas
- Configuration patterns
- Admin tips
- Product usage best practices

<Callout kind="tip">
  If you post in the community, add context up front. The more specific your question, the faster someone can help.
</Callout>

### How to ask a useful question

1. State the goal in one sentence.
2. Describe what you have already tried.
3. Mention the product area involved.
4. Include a screenshot or example if it helps.
5. Follow up if someone asks for more detail.

## Enterprise account management

If you manage a larger Zendesk rollout, your account team can help with planning, adoption, and operational guidance. This path is useful for organizations that need coordinated support across teams, regions, or business units.

Enterprise account management usually helps with:

- Rollout planning
- Service governance
- Security and trust questions
- Adoption planning
- Billing and contract coordination

### What to prepare for account reviews

Bring a short summary of your goals, current usage, and any open risks. If you are preparing for a review, it also helps to note which teams use Zendesk and which workflows matter most.

### Support path at a glance

| Need | Best option |
| --- | --- |
| A bug or account issue | Submit a support ticket |
| Advice from other users | Ask the community |
| Rollout or governance guidance | Contact your account team |
| General usage help | Search the help center first |

## How to get help faster

The fastest support cases are clear, complete, and easy to reproduce. Before you send anything, make sure your message answers three questions: what happened, where it happened, and what you expected instead.
