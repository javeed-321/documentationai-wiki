## Overview

Channels keep conversations organized so your team finds what they need quickly. Whether you need a public space for company updates or a private area for sensitive projects, Slack gives you control over visibility and access. This guide walks through the most common tasks so you can set up and maintain channels without frustration.

## Creating Channels

Open the channel list and click the plus icon next to Channels. Give the channel a clear name and add a short description that explains its purpose. Choose whether the channel should be public or private before you create it.

<Callout kind="tip">
  Use descriptive names like project-updates or team-onboarding so members know exactly where to post.
</Callout>

## Privacy Settings

Public channels appear in search and anyone in your workspace can join. Private channels stay hidden and require an invitation.

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Public" icon="globe">
    Anyone can browse and join without approval. Great for company-wide announcements.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Private" icon="lock">
    Only invited members see the channel. Use for HR discussions or client work.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## Archiving and Deleting

When a project ends, archive the channel so history stays searchable but no new messages appear. Go to Channel settings and select Archive. If you need to remove a channel completely, choose Delete instead.

<Expandable title="What happens when you archive?">
  Archived channels remain readable by members who had access. You can unarchive later if the topic becomes active again.
</Expandable>

## Channel Permissions

Workspace owners and admins set default rules. For individual channels, open settings and adjust who can post, invite others, or manage topics.

<Card title="Quick permission check" icon="shield" href="#channel-permissions">
  Review settings monthly to keep the right people in control.
</Card>

## Examples

- A public #announcements channel shares company news with everyone.
- A private #executive-planning channel limits access to leadership.

## Best Practices

Keep channel names short and consistent. Archive channels that have been inactive for 90 days. Remind members to use threads instead of creating new channels for every small topic.
